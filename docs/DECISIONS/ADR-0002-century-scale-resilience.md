# ADR-0002 — Design for century-scale adaptability and stewardship

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 23 November 2026
- **Decision owner:** To be confirmed
- **Scope:** In X Evolution

## Context

In X Evolution has expressed an ambition to consider the next 100 years. No organisation can reliably forecast that period's technology, markets, laws or social conditions. Treating a current vendor, application or business model as permanent would create avoidable dependency and continuity risk.

## Decision

Adopt a 100-year longevity framework based on stable purpose and values, replaceable business and technology components, durable records, succession, financial discipline, security agility and responsible exit planning.

Plan operational work through short, evidence-based horizons. Review longer-horizon assumptions periodically rather than treating a century plan as a fixed schedule.

## Options considered

1. Predict a detailed 100-year product and technology roadmap.
2. Ignore the century horizon and plan only near-term features.
3. Define enduring principles and renewal mechanisms, with rolling near-term plans.

## Rationale

Option 3 is more robust under uncertainty. It separates durable institutional responsibilities from technologies and business activities that must change.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Reduces key-person, vendor and format lock-in.
- Encourages recoverable, documented and transferable knowledge.
- Makes leadership succession and responsible wind-down explicit.
- Creates a basis for periodic security and archive migration.

### Trade-offs
- Requires recurring review and maintenance.
- Long-term preservation and redundancy have real costs.
- The organisation must avoid overengineering before its scale and obligations justify controls.

## Design references

- ISO 14721:2025, OAIS reference model.
- NIST IR 8547 (2026), post-quantum cryptography transition guidance.

## Revisit when

The legal structure, first business line, operating scale, data-retention obligations and technology stack are approved, and at least every five years thereafter.
